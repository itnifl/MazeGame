# Approved Requirements

- SR-1: Add an automated cross-frontend movement parity test that replays the same enemy seeds and world snapshots for WANDER, PATROL, and AGGRESSIVE modes, and fails if one frontend reintroduces small-area movement loops that the other does not reproduce.
- SR-2: Define `/showenemypath` and `/sep` as a live runtime-path overlay requirement. The overlay shall show the exact active path buffer an enemy is currently following in AGGRESSIVE path-follow and PATROL path modes, and shall not substitute a newly computed shortest path to the player.
- SR-3: Define WANDER collision behavior explicitly. A wander enemy shall keep moving in its current direction until blocked, then choose a random passable cardinal direction, including the immediate reverse direction when that is the random result or the only passable option.
- SR-4: The terminal overlay (opened with T) shall be able to consume the Esc key to close itself. The global Esc handler that pauses/exits the game shall not intercept Esc while the terminal is active.
- SR-5: All enemy-state service instances (PatrolMovementService, AdaptiveAggressiveMovementService, AntiLoopWanderMovementService) shall be fully reset when a new game session starts, ensuring no state leaks from a previous session.
- SR-6: The frontend dispose/cleanup method shall stop and release all running timers (including path-overlay and HUD-message timers) to prevent resource leaks and spurious post-dispose callbacks.
- SR-7: Wall render thickness shall be a single shared constant (`StageConstants.WallThicknessPx`) consumed by all rendering frontends. No frontend may hard-code a wall thickness value; a CI check (or unit assertion) shall verify both frontends read the same value.
- SR-8: Ghost tangibility (phasing) must be fully implemented in both JavaFX and libGDX frontends. A phasing ghost (nonTangibilityEnergy > 0) must pass through walls, be rendered semi-transparent with opacity `clamp(1 − energy/100 + 0.1, 0.1, 1.0)`, and DOES deal contact damage to the player. Only wall-collision checks are skipped during phasing; damage is not suppressed. Automated parity tests shall assert that both frontends behave identically for the same initial energy value. [IMPLEMENTED in GhostNonTangibilityService and GhostPhasingMovementService]
- SR-9: The path-hint P-key feature shall enforce a per-difficulty time budget (Easy: 45 s, Normal: 25 s, Hard: 15 s). Once the budget is exhausted the hint shall become unavailable for the rest of the game and the player shall be shown a clear HUD message. The same budget rules shall apply in both JavaFX and libGDX.
- SR-10: Enemy-player collision detection shall use axis-aligned bounding-box intersection as the primary contact test for all enemy types except phasing ghosts. No additional Euclidean center-distance guard shall be applied on top of the bounding-box test, as this creates false negatives on diagonal approaches.
- SR-11: All shared gameplay constants (board dimensions, character sizes, wall thickness, touch distance, speed defaults) shall reside in `StageConstants` inside the `main.game.maze.mazeworld` module. Constants must never be duplicated or overridden in individual frontend modules.
- SR-12: Enemy damage shall be blocked whenever any maze wall separates the enemy centre from the player centre, regardless of bounding-box overlap. This rule applies to all tangible enemy types in both the JavaFX and libGDX frontends. The wall-crossing test shall use `WallCollisionUtil.wallBetween` / `wallBetweenVectors` and must be covered by unit tests.
- SR-13: Projectile splash damage (e.g. PumpkinBomber) shall also be subject to wall-blocking: if the explosion origin is on the opposite side of a wall from the player, the blast shall not deal damage. This is a separate, deferred concern from melee contact damage.
- SR-14: The per-waypoint dwell time feature (F22: `path.time` in XMI, `N ms` in DSL) is currently not implemented. When implemented, `PatrolMovementService` shall pause the enemy at each waypoint for the configured duration before computing the next leg of the patrol path. Both frontends must honour the dwell time consistently.
- SR-15: Ghost phasing activation trigger should be configurable via the XMI model (e.g., a `nonTangibilityTrigger` attribute). Currently energy is set by game logic directly; externalizing the trigger to the model would allow designers to configure which events cause a ghost to enter phasing mode without code changes.
- SR-16: Add a visual indicator to the HUD when a phasing ghost is active nearby, to give the player a clear signal that a ghost currently bypasses walls and may approach from unexpected directions.
- SR-17: The `GhostPhasingMovementService` direction map grows unbounded over a session when many ghost enemies are present. Add a cleanup hook so entries for dead or despawned enemies are removed to prevent memory growth in long sessions.
- SR-18: The `maze-javafx-backend` module and all future JavaFX-specific modules shall be validated by a dedicated integration test that starts the `App` in headless mode (TestFX or `monocle`), loads a level, and asserts that the game loop starts without exceptions. This guards against silent regressions when dependencies shift between modules.
- SR-19: `ScreenNameFXMLConstants` in `maze-javafx` shall be validated by a unit test that asserts every constant value is a valid resource path resolvable by `getClass().getResource(...)` at runtime. This prevents typo regressions after FXML files are moved between modules.
- SR-20: Module membership boundaries shall be enforced by a ArchUnit or Moduliths test. The rule: no class in `maze-common-backend` may import from `javafx.*` or `main.game.maze.libgdx.*`; no class in `maze-common-frontend` may import from `main.game.maze.libgdx.*`. This prevents future accidental re-contamination.
- SR-22: Introduce a mandatory shared game session aggregate adoption rule. Both JavaFX and libGDX shall use `GameSession` and `GameMode` from `maze-common-backend` as the only source of truth for mode transitions and session flags (pause, loading, win-save state), and no frontend shall keep parallel duplicate mode state fields once migration is complete.
- SR-23: Add a rendering parity contract test for extracted libGDX views (`GdxGameWorldView`, `GdxHudView`, `GdxOverlayView`) that verifies world overlays and HUD anchor positions remain equivalent to pre-extraction behavior across window resize and difficulty changes.
- SR-24: libGDX frontend bootstrap shall use a `Game` root with `setScreen(...)` routing instead of launching gameplay directly as an `ApplicationAdapter`. During migration, a compatibility adapter may wrap the legacy gameplay class, but all new frontend modes must be introduced as `Screen` implementations.
- SR-25: Texture and media loading in libGDX shall be centralized in a dedicated AssetManager boundary service. Frontend gameplay screens must request assets through this service only; direct `new Texture(...)` creation in screens is forbidden once migration is complete.
- SR-26: libGDX screens shall receive shared dependencies through a single `GameContext` boundary object rather than constructing services directly. New screens must extend a common base screen abstraction to standardize lifecycle behavior, reduce duplication, and improve testability.
- SR-27: Start menu responsibilities in libGDX shall be owned by a dedicated `MenuScreenController`. The menu screen may temporarily route to a compatibility gameplay screen during migration, but difficulty selection and start-loading flow must not remain in the gameplay screen once full extraction is complete.
- SR-28: Active gameplay responsibilities in libGDX shall be owned by a dedicated `PlayScreenController`. During migration, `PlayScreenController` may delegate to legacy behavior through an adapter, but update and draw orchestration must ultimately move out of `GdxGameScreenController`.
- SR-29: Difficulty naming, board dimensions, and board-size labels shall be centralized in dedicated collaborators (`DifficultyBoardConfig` in libGDX plus shared `DifficultyPresentationSupport` in backend modules, or equivalent boundaries) consumed by both `MenuScreenController` and gameplay orchestration. Frontend classes must not duplicate difficulty-switch logic once migration is complete.
- SR-30: Visual style configuration loading in libGDX shall follow one shared policy boundary (`GdxVisualStyleSupport` or equivalent): attempt XMI first, then properties, then `MazeVisualStyleConfig.DEFAULT`. Screens and launcher entry points must not duplicate try-catch loading logic.
- SR-31: Terminal command interpretation in libGDX shall use a shared command support boundary (`GdxTerminalCommandSupport` or equivalent) that maps raw command text to deterministic command outcomes. Gameplay screens may own only the runtime state effects, not duplicated parsing and command mapping rules.
- SR-32: Temporary debug-overlay timer state in libGDX (behaviour label visibility, movement label visibility, enemy path overlay duration) shall be owned by a dedicated runtime state collaborator (`GdxDebugOverlayState` or equivalent). Gameplay screens shall consume this state but shall not duplicate timer bookkeeping logic.
- SR-33: Current-score calculation and high-score persistence in libGDX shall be routed through a shared score support boundary (`GdxScoreSupport` or equivalent). Gameplay screens may coordinate when score changes happen, but score math and repository access shall not be duplicated inline.
- SR-34: Win name entry, trimming, backspace handling, and save preparation in libGDX shall be routed through a shared win score support boundary (`GdxWinScoreSupport` or equivalent). Gameplay screens may own the UI event flow, but name sanitization and save readiness checks shall not be duplicated inline.
- SR-35: libGDX gameplay input shall be handled by a key binding registry that maps logical actions to command objects. Key polling shall be centralized in one input snapshot reader and action dispatch shall run through a router to avoid scattered keyboard checks in gameplay screens.
- SR-36: libGDX gameplay mutable runtime state shall be exposed through a dedicated model boundary so that controller classes coordinate behavior without directly owning every gameplay field.
- SR-37: libGDX frame rendering shall be orchestrated by a dedicated render pipeline that consumes a render snapshot and delegates actual drawing to existing view classes.
- SR-38: libGDX mode specific updates shall be routed through a dedicated mode router that evaluates mode handlers in a deterministic order and returns after the first handling controller.
- SR-39: libGDX gameplay start and reset flow shall be encapsulated in a dedicated bootstrap boundary that initializes arena, runtime model, combat state, and per session runtime flags in one place.
- SR-40: libGDX audio transitions for menu, in game, win, and game over shall be encapsulated behind a dedicated coordinator boundary to keep screen controllers free from direct channel switching policy.
- SR-41: `GdxGameScreenController` construction shall be migrated away from the current telescoping-constructor chain (multiple overloads with four consecutive boolean parameters: `ownsAssetService`, `autoStartOnCreate`, `immediateStartOnCreate`, `showHighScoresOnCreate`). Replace it with a single parameter object / builder (e.g. `GdxGameScreenOptions`) so call sites are self-documenting and adding a new flag does not require touching every overload. This addresses the long-parameter-list / boolean-parameter code smell (CRR-15). Status: implemented. `GdxGameScreenOptions` (immutable options plus fluent builder) now backs a single canonical `GdxGameScreenController(GdxGameScreenOptions)` constructor; all call sites use the builder, and `GdxGameScreenOptionsTest` covers the boundary.
- SR-200: PumpkinBomber LOB projectiles shall arc over walls toward the target position. In-flight wall collisions must only apply to STRAIGHT projectiles. LOB projectiles must not detonate on wall contact and shall resolve damage only at splash impact. This behavior is required in both JavaFX and libGDX frontends.
- SR-133: The bomb flame explosion shall deal up to 100 HP per cardinal direction in both frontends. Enemies, the player, and breakable walls are all valid targets in each direction. Targets are processed in ascending distance order from the bomb origin. A breakable wall that exhausts the remaining budget stops the flame; a destroyed wall allows the flame to continue past it and the nav-graph is rewired automatically. The player is a pass-through target: they receive the current remaining budget as damage at their position but neither block nor reduce the flame's budget for the targets that follow. Indestructible walls always stop the flame.
- SR-134: The bomb flame corridor shall span the full width of one maze cell (approximately 30 px half-width on each side of the direction axis) so that targets in an adjacent corridor are not hit and so that the blast visually fills the corridor. Both frontends must use `StageConstants.NaviGraphStepSize` (= 30) as the corridor half-width.
- SR-135: Both frontends shall display the bomb inventory counter and the score counter in the same screen corner (top-right) and the two labels must not overlap. In JavaFX the `scoreHudContainer` already holds both stacked vertically. In libGDX the `GdxHudView` score panel height is extended to 54 px to fit two text rows, and the bombs label is drawn inside the score panel box instead of the top-left corner.
- SR-136: `GameMazeWorld.assignBreakableWalls` shall accept an empty material list as a valid call meaning "no breakable walls; all walls are solid". Callers must be able to transition a world to fully-solid state without being required to hold at least one dummy material entry.
- SR-239: Items spawn every 3 s (until the cap is reached) at random corridor points away from the player, capped at 5 (easy), 10 (medium) or 15 (hard / large maps); spawning resumes when the player picks items up. Item kinds and numbers are defined in the `items` model (`items.ecore` / `items.xmi`). Both frontends.
- SR-240: A bomb pickup gives one more bomb.
- SR-241: A laser rifle pickup gives 5 shots. Tab switches to the rifle, which the player holds in the hands; it fires along the facing and cannot harm steel walls.
- SR-242: A sledgehammer pickup gives 5 swings that strike nearby enemies; it cannot harm walls. A swing looks like a giant swing.
- SR-243: A shield power cube blocks all damage to the player for 5 seconds.
- SR-244: Small hearts restore part of the player's health.
- SR-245: Enemies hit by the laser or the sledgehammer react according to the damage taken; a shot reacts differently from a blow.
- SR-246: Picking up each kind of item makes a distinct sound.
- SR-247: Killing an enemy raises the killing player's score in proportion to the enemy's danger; who killed what is recorded per player for future multiplayer.
- SR-256: Items are drawn as large as the player (the items model's `itemSizeFactor`, 1.0 x the player size), with sprite art that fills its frame.
- SR-257: Items never spawn inside or over a wall: only corridor points inside the maze that keep half an item plus half a wall away from every wall are used.
- SR-258: Killed enemies that can come back wait 15 seconds before they respawn (both frontends; the opponent model's `resurrectionTime`).
- SR-259: Picking up a heart flashes the player, shows a green arrow rising over the player's head and plays an 8-bit "extra life" jingle.
- SR-263: A weapons HUD in the top-left corner shows every weapon the player has (with its stock) and the one in hand highlighted; Tab moves the highlight. The player's lives are shown next to it. Both frontends.
- SR-264: The player has lives: 6 on easy, 5 on normal (medium) and 3 on hard, from the difficulties model (`startingLives`).
- SR-265: An extra-life item spawns with the other items (rarer than them); picking it up gives one more life.
- SR-266: When the player dies with lives left, they respawn at their start position with full health (after the death is shown, with a short spawn protection); the game is over only when the last life is lost.
- SR-267: Besides the arrows, Space and Tab: Q moves left, W forward (up), E right, D down; a right click uses the weapon in hand (shoot, swing or drop a bomb). A keeps moving left and S down; D now moves down instead of right.
- SR-274: After winning level 1 the player can go on to level 2 (a Next level choice on the win screen); both frontends. JavaFX: a Next level button on the win screen; libGDX: a Next Level button on the win overlay. A new game (menu or after game over) starts on level 1 again.
- SR-275: The levels are defined in the levels model (`xmi/levels/levels.ecore` + `levels.xmi`, dynamic EMF): wall zones, threat capacity and the sergeant with its numbers per difficulty. A level with zones has exactly four, each weaker than the one outside it; the loader fails fast otherwise.
- SR-276: Level 2 is still a generated maze (the same generator and navigation graph); only the wall materials are assigned by zone. The maze is divided into concentric zones around the heart (level 2 has four).
- SR-277: In level 2 the outer zone has the strongest walls and the inner zone (around the heart) the weakest: fewer breakable walls and tougher materials outside, more and weaker ones inside.
- SR-278: Level 2 has a higher threat capacity than level 1: the difficulty's maximum threat x the level's `threatCapacityFactor` (1.5).
- SR-279: New enemy, the zombie sergeant: it never moves, and fires a burst of gatling-gun rounds at the player whenever it sees them (within its sight range, no wall in between), rests, and fires again while the player stays in sight. Its numbers are in the levels model.
- SR-280: Sergeants stand close to the heart: six on hard, four on normal (medium), one on easy.
- SR-281: A killed sergeant drops its gatling gun; the player picks it up and can use it like the other weapons (Tab to select, attack key to fire a burst along the facing). The gun never spawns as a normal item.
- SR-282: The player inherits their weapons (stock and weapon in hand) from the previous level; retrying a level starts with the weapons it was entered with.
- SR-290: The wall zones of a level are visible: a level can draw every wall with the sprite of its material (glass, dirt, wood, stone, steel; the model's `showWallMaterials`), in both frontends, so the player sees the strong outer zone and the weak inner zone.
- SR-291: Levels are reusable data: a level is one element of `levels.xmi` with any number of zones, its own threat capacity, wall look, weapon inheritance and optional sergeants; the player can play any number of levels in order, and `docs/level-authoring.md` describes how to make one without Java.
- SR-292: The gatling gun (the sergeants' and the one the player picks up) looks like a spray of separate bullets with glowing tracers and sparks where they land, not a laser beam, in both frontends; its sound is a gatling gun (spinning motor under a fast stream of shots).
- SR-293: Breaking one wall never brings down a large part of a level: no single broken wall may take more than 10 % of the maze with it (the cascade rule, GR-36), so every zone of a level keeps solid walls.
- SR-294: A level can limit how far a wall collapse spreads (`maxCascadeDepth` in the levels model; level 2: two steps), so walls cascade a little but a broken wall never brings down much of the maze, while every wall can still be broken by a bomb.
- SR-295: The background of the normal difficulty is a lush meadow with patches of burning grass (scorched earth, flames, embers, smoke), in both frontends; it tiles without a seam. The easy and hard backgrounds are unchanged.
- SR-296: The background of the easy difficulty is friendly light woods: a bright, dappled forest floor with moss, ferns, fallen leaves, twigs, tiny mushrooms and flowers, nothing dark or burning; both frontends; it tiles without a seam.
- SR-297: When the player's bomb goes off it makes a deep explosion sound and shakes the screen (strongly, for about 0.7 seconds, dying away); both frontends.
- SR-298: Level 3 is level 2 plus new hazards: its zones, wall look, cascade limit, sergeants and threat capacity are taken from level 2 (`baseLevel` in the levels model).
- SR-299: In level 3 a circle of three anti-personnel mines surrounds every sergeant. A mine is an olive-drab army mine with a faint light-blue pulsating circle round it. Mines only lie on free ground (clear of walls, visible from their sergeant).
- SR-300: A player who touches a mine sets it off: the player takes a little damage (20), walls and enemies in the small blast take a little less (15), and the mine leaves a hole in the ground that stays. A player who falls into the hole is teleported to the opposite edge of the map, onto free ground.
- SR-301: Level 3 has a new ghost, the portal ghost: it does not hurt the player; when it touches them they are teleported to a random spot at the outer edge of the map, never inside a wall. There are 10 on hard, 6 on normal (medium) and 3 on easy, in addition to everything level 2 has.
- SR-302: After any teleport the player cannot be teleported again for 2.5 seconds (the level's teleport rules), so a second ghost or hole cannot send them straight back.
- SR-308: When the player is teleported they flash in at the new place (a white flash, a column of light, a ring of light), the screen briefly says "Teleported" in big letters and a deep voice says "Teleported"; both frontends. The word, how long it stays and the sounds are part of the level model (`TeleportRules`).
- SR-309: Behaviour that both frontends need lives once in the shared backend and is data driven from the Ecore level model: the level's hazards (`HazardSession`: mines, holes, falling, teleport exits, cooldown), where its enemies go (`LevelPlacement`), what a teleport does (`TeleportRules`) and how a banner fades (`BannerFade`). A frontend only converts coordinates, draws and plays.
- SR-312: The levels are written in the Xtext DSL (`xmi/levels/levels.mazegame`, the `level` section of `MazeDsl.xtext`) and the game's `levels.xmi` is generated from it by `MazeDslGenerator.levelsXmi`; a test (`MazeDslLevelsTest`) fails when the shipped XMI is out of step with the DSL source or invalid against `levels.ecore`.
- SR-313: Level 4 is level 3 plus a second circle of mines and guard towers: its zones, sergeants, portal ghosts, teleport rules and threat capacity come from level 3 (`baseLevel`).
- SR-314: Level 4 has a second circle of mines round every sergeant, at a distance from the inner circle, with as many mines as the inner circle and staggered half a step from it, so the mines on the map are doubled (`MineSpec.secondRingRadius`, `MineField.place`). The second circle follows the same rules as the first: free ground, visible from the sergeant.
- SR-315: Level 4 has guard towers on the outer parts of the map: 4 on hard, 3 on medium (normal), 2 on easy. They stand on free ground in the outer band, as far apart as the maze allows, away from the player's start (`LevelPlacement.towerPosts`); they never move.
- SR-316: A guard tower shoots over walls, but only up to 3 walls away: it fires only while the player is within its range and no more than 3 walls lie between them (`WallCrossing`, `GuardTowerGun.canSee`).
- SR-317: A guard tower can be blown up (bombs, lasers, sledgehammers, mine and rocket blasts) but takes a whole lot of damage (1500 hit points, over seven times a sergeant). When it takes damage it looks damaged (the healthy, damaged, bleeding and tired sprites, by the usual damage-state rule); when it is destroyed it blows up, leaves no corpse and does not come back.
- SR-318: A guard tower fires an rpg every 3 seconds and fires its gatling gun in between: a burst of six rounds starting halfway between two rpgs (`GuardTowerGun`). The rocket flies at 300 px/s to where the player was and explodes there: it damages the player within its blast (shield permitting) and walls and enemies in the blast like any explosion.
- SR-319: The right sounds play for the events: an rpg launch whoosh when a rocket leaves, an rpg explosion when it lands, the gatling burst sound for the gatling, a stone knock when a tower is hit, a long collapse together with the deep bomb boom and screen shake when a tower is destroyed; both frontends. The sounds are part of the level model (`GuardTower`) and are made by `tools/sound-gen/SoundEffectGenerator.java`.
- SR-320: What the towers do is decided once in the shared backend (`GuardTowerSession`, `GuardTowerGun`, `WallCrossing`, `LevelPlacement.towerPosts`) from the `GuardTower` element of the Ecore level model and the level written in the Xtext DSL (`guardTower`, `towers`, `secondRing`); each frontend only feeds it the towers and the player, draws and plays (JavaFX `FxGuardTowerController`, `GuardTowerSpawner`, `GuardTowerCharacter`; libGDX `GdxGuardTowerSupport`, `RuntimeVisualModelLoader.guardTowerSpawns`).
